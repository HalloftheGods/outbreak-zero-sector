# Undead of Night: Last Round

A Hall of the Gods, Inc. game. A top-down pixel art zombie survival game set in an open town at night. Play solo in the browser, or co-op over a small WebSocket server. Ammo and health are finite: you find them in the town's buildings, and every gunshot tells nearby zombies where you are.

## How it plays

- **The town** is 3200x3200 pixels with five districts (Downtown, Riverside, Industrial Yard, Old Town, Park). It is generated from a seed and fenced in. Each building has a real room behind one south door, and the roof fades when you walk in.
- **Loot** sits in buildings by type: medical supplies in pharmacies and clinics, guns and ammo at the police station and gun shop. The carbine is always at the police station and the shotgun at the gun shop. Each gun takes its own ammo, and spare rounds have a cap.
- **Zombies** roam the town from the start. They notice you by sight, which walls block, and by noise, which gunfire spreads.
- **Hordes** come after a scavenging lull: 140 seconds before the first and 100 seconds between the rest. A horde lasts up to 60 seconds, and any survivors go back to roaming.
- **Levels** run from 1 to 10, each its own town with its own look. A title card names the level and how many hordes it takes. Once the last one is seen off, a banner says the van has come, an amber arrow on screen and a marker on the town map point to it, and the HUD says to get there. Reduced motion stops the falling weather and the pulsing light.
- **Searching**: hold E beside cars, boxes (crates), shelves, or furniture (drawers, lockers, fridges, cabinets) to go through them, from one second for a drawer to two for a car or locker. Moving or taking a hit undoes the search and it wants a fresh press. What was inside falls at your feet, a container stays open for everyone, and about one search in ten knocks something over, which zombies within 170 pixels hear. Most containers hold nothing, so searching stays a choice, but every home has a find in it somewhere. The ammo budget counts floors and containers together, and every town settles on it with half in each, topped up or trimmed at every level and difficulty. Containers follow from the world map and interiors in `shared/containers.ts`, their contents in `shared/container-loot.ts` and the rules in `shared/container-search.ts`.
- **The Radio** is a find in a searched container: about one car in ten still has a working one, and some towns also hold one, never more than one, left in a house, apartment, diner or garage. None takes the place of ammo. Which containers follow from the town's seed alone, on a generator of its own (`shared/radio.ts`), so the simulation's rolls do not move. The first one picked up shows a banner, and any pickup plays a short static-and-tone sound, and unlocks the title's Sound Test in that browser. In co-op the pickup event reaches every connected client, so the whole party unlocks together. The unlock is saved apart from the level progress (`src/game/sound-unlock.ts`), survives reloads and old saves, and the simulation never reads it.
- **Sound Test**: the title menu shows ??? in its place until a Radio is found, and the page then says only that a radio is out there somewhere. Once open it lists the music by name for levels reached and ??? for the rest (the title and horde tracks belong to level 1, and the victory and credits tracks open on finishing the game), plus every sound effect. Lists are read from `src/audio/playlists.ts` and the sound table, so a new track or sound shows up on its own. One thing plays at a time: select to play, select again or Stop to stop. Music goes through the soundtrack and mixer, so volume and mute apply, and the title music returns when the page closes. The lists and unlock rules are in `src/game/sound-test.ts`.
- **Town rules**: three themes change play as well as the look, from one table in `shared/town-rules.ts`, and the level card names the rule. Floodwater (level 4) slows walking outdoors to 80%, buildings stay dry. Blackout (level 6) cuts how far zombies see to 70% but every noise carries 30% further. Fresh snow (level 8) keeps a print every 9 ticks of walking or sprinting outdoors for 20 seconds, and a roamer that crosses one follows it to whoever left it; sneaking leaves none (`shared/snow-tracks.ts`).
- **Bottles and car alarms**: everyone starts a run with one empty bottle and can carry three. G throws one at the crosshair, up to 170 pixels and short of the first wall; it breaks at once and every zombie within 220 pixels of the smash comes to look, which pulls them off you. An empty-handed search turns up a bottle 35% of the time. Going through a car sets its alarm off one time in eight, a 420-pixel noise at the car. Rules in `shared/bottles.ts`.
- **Screamers** roam from level 3 (3% of roamers, half a percent more each level, never in a horde). They are walkers recoloured grey and violet, frail (45 health) and worth 30. One that spots a survivor wails, a noise every zombie within 300 pixels hears, then needs 10 seconds before it can call again (`scream` in `shared/zombie-senses.ts`).
- **Sneak and sprint**: hold C to sneak at 55% speed, spotted from 60% as far; hold Shift to sprint 40% faster, with footsteps heard 110 pixels away every half second.
- **Out of ammo?** Shove. It makes no noise and pushes back the two nearest zombies. A zombie you just shoved keeps its footing for a moment, so standing still and shoving will not hold off a crowd or a brute.
- **The knife** turns up in diners, houses, garages and containers. With one the shove becomes Shove & Stab: 18 damage instead of 4, just as silent, with the same two-target limit and stagger immunity. It comes with you from town to town and is lost when you die.
- **Survivors**: pick one of five on the title screen, and the choice is remembered. Night is harder for zombies to see (they spot her from three quarters as far), Khronus8 reloads in three quarters of the time, Satori runs 12% faster, Rex shoves half again as hard, and Sol's kills drop loot twice as often, with about one empty container in seven turning something up anyway. The traits are one table in `shared/characters.ts`. In co-op nobody shares a survivor: you get yours if it is free, otherwise the next free one, and teammates' tags and HUD chips name who they are playing.
- **Co-op** keeps your run if you refresh: you come back with the same health, guns and spot, and a dead teammate still waits for the next horde. A newcomer after the first horde starts with half health and no spares. If the last player standing leaves, even to refresh, the run ends. Play Again restarts once everyone has asked, or 15 seconds after the first person asked.
- **Difficulty** has four modes, picked on the setup page and remembered: Night Light On, Stay Close (the default), Lights Out and Undead Tired. Each scales the ammo in town, zombie speed and bite, horde size and the lull between hordes. Lights Out is the balance the game was tuned on. In co-op the first player into an empty room sets it, and the results show it. The table is `shared/difficulty.ts`.
- **The workbench** opens after each van ride but the last. Barrels, grips, magazines and suppressors from the gun shop, police station, garages and warehouses buy upgrades within a gun's slots: damage, fire rate, magazine size, reload speed, or a suppressor that cuts its noise radius by 65%. A rare toolkit moves one upgrade between guns. A found gun can carry a quirk, good and bad at once. Upgrades stay on the gun across levels and go with it on death, and no build passes 1.5 times stock damage per second. Solo waits until you are ready; co-op gives everyone on their feet 45 seconds.
- **Special rounds** are a separate slot from upgrades: a gun holds at most one effect. Round kits (tins with a mark on the lid) turn up mostly on floors and sometimes in searched containers, three or four a town, and a player carries at most two of each. Fitting one at the workbench uses it and replaces any effect already on the gun, after a "Replace X with Y?" confirm; a toolkit moves an effect to a gun that has none. *Flame rounds* set a zombie alight for 3 seconds (18 damage), let a burning zombie light one that touches it, and the fire is loud and glows. *Acid rounds* stack corrosion up to 3 times for 5 seconds. A shotgun blast adds one stack however many pellets land, and each stack makes that zombie take 10% more damage (15% on a brute) from acid rounds and from burns, not from plain guns, so a teammate's acid never lifts another gun past the power cap. *Piercing rounds* go through the first zombie into the next at half damage, and walls still stop them. *Tranq rounds* slow a zombie for 3 seconds (less on brutes) and hit 30% lighter. Effects only apply to ranged hits, credit the shooter, and the gun's direct damage gives way so no build passes the power cap. Status lives on the zombie and clears with the level; carried guns keep their effect. The HUD names the active gun's effect next to its ammo, and the gun's pips show a coloured effect pip. On screen a burning zombie shows pixel flames and a glow, an acid-hit one a green wash with drips, a slowed one a blue wash, and effect rounds leave a coloured tracer trail. Reduced motion freezes the flames and drips, and flames are capped per frame (`MAX_FLAMES`).
- **Starting at a level**: on the Levels page, a level you have reached can be picked to start a solo run there. The setup page then shows "Start at level N: title", and a level that is not unlocked, a stale saved value or an unknown number falls back to a normal run from level 1. With nothing picked, Play continues from the furthest level reached. Co-op always starts on level 1, and the setup page says so. The rules are in `src/game/level-progress.ts` and the words in `src/game/level-select.ts`.
- **Solo** pauses when the window loses focus.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| WASD | Move |
| Shift (hold) | Sprint: 40% faster, footsteps heard 110 px away |
| C (hold) | Sneak: a bit over half speed, spotted from 60% as far |
| Mouse | Aim, hold to fire |
| F or right mouse | Shove & Stab |
| E (hold) | Search furniture |
| R | Reload |
| H | Use a medkit |
| G | Throw a bottle at the crosshair |
| 1, 2, 3, Q | Weapons |
| Tab or N | Town map |
| Esc | Pause |
| M | Mute |

## Architecture

- **Simulation**: `shared/` holds the deterministic `GameSimulation` at 30 ticks a second. The browser runs it directly for solo, and the server runs the same code for co-op. Town generation is in `shared/world-map.ts` and the `town-*` files, loot in `shared/loot.ts`, zombie senses in `shared/zombie-senses.ts`, gun upgrades in `shared/gun-upgrade-data.ts` and `shared/gun-upgrades.ts`, and the hold between towns in `shared/workbench.ts`. A `GameSimulation` only stops at the bench when its `workbench` option says so: solo passes `hold`, the server `timed`.
- **Server**: `server/server.ts` is an authoritative `ws` server. Each client gets its own snapshot from `shared/snapshot-view.ts`, with zombies and pickups within `VIEWER_RANGE` (600px) of that player, or around every player for a dead viewer. Close codes: 4000 room full, 4001 server busy, 4002 no join within 10 seconds. A link too slow for the snapshots falls behind rather than catching up.
- **Client**: `src/game/` owns the run and the HUD state, `src/engine/` renders to a low-resolution back buffer (`BuildingLayer` for shells and rooms, `LightField` for the dithered lighting), and `src/network/` holds the local and socket sessions, with snapshot interpolation and local prediction. `src/game/run-stage.ts` watches each snapshot's `mapSeed` and `level`: a new town rebuilds the world, renderer, town map and prediction before that snapshot's events run, and `src/engine/level-look.ts` holds each level's light, tint and weather.
- **Art**: `src/art/` builds every sprite as pure RGBA data, with no image files. Render contact sheets with `node --experimental-strip-types tools/contact-sheet.ts <sheet> out.png` (`kits` shows the four round kits as pickups and icons). Status drawing lives in `src/engine/status-fx.ts` and works from the snapshot alone, so nothing lingers across a reconnect; the effect sparks are in `status-bursts.ts` and the sound recipes in `src/audio/sfx-effects.ts`. The workbench rows for effects are in `src/game/bench-effects.ts` and `WorkbenchEffect.vue`.
- **Audio**: `src/audio/` synthesizes every sound effect, the wind and the distant groans with Web Audio. Music is recorded tracks by ExoticService in `public/music/` (128-192 kbps MP3), which `soundtrack.ts` streams through the mixer: *Silent Ruins (Variation)* on the title, the lull of each town from `playlists.ts` (with rotating alternate takes across all 10 levels), and *Horde Attack* and its alternate in turn for hordes. Game over has no music. The Sound Test plays tracks through `Soundtrack.audition()` and sounds through `AudioEngine.previewSfx()`. A won run plays *Dark Salvation* on `VictoryScreen.vue`, then `CreditsScreen.vue` (lines in `src/game/credits.ts`) rolls to *Surviving the Night*, and any key or click skips to the title; `EndingFlow.vue` runs the two. If a track will not load, the old generated music takes over.
- **UI**: `src/components/` holds the studio splash, title screen, HUD, banners, town map and game over screen. Any key or click skips the splash, and `?nosplash` in the address turns it off. The title is the logo, `TitleMenu.vue` and the credit; the items and the words on its pages live in `src/game/title-menu.ts`. Each page sits in `TitlePage.vue`, which gives it the panel, heading, Back and Esc, and the title scales on its own unit, `--title-u`. Enter only starts a run on the setup page's Start or Join: in a field it moves focus to that button.
- **How to Play** is a printed magazine, *Out of Town*, in `src/components/magazine/`, with the Town Survival Bureau as its publisher. `src/game/how-to-play.ts` holds the rules and stays the only source of them. `magazine-pages.ts` builds the issue from those sections, `magazine-hints.ts` says how each section is laid out, and `how-to-play-visuals.ts` and `magazine-photos.ts` supply the pictures, posing the game's own sprites on its own tiles (`town` is a real clip of a generated town). A section with no hint gets the default spread, and a point a hint does not place is added to its first article, so new rules always print. `magazine-fit.ts` keeps a page from overflowing: it shrinks the type, then drops blocks marked `extra`, then shrinks again. `magazine-controller.ts` handles turning: Prev and Next, chapter links, the arrow and page keys, and a swipe, with a 3D page turn or a plain swap under reduced motion. A window taller than it is wide reads one page at a time.
- **Analytics** live in `src/game/analytics/` and are on by default, with no prompt. `index.html` has no Google tag: `sender.ts` injects it at runtime unless the player turned Analytics off in Options. A saved off stays off, and a Global Privacy Control signal counts as off (Options shows it off and disabled). The choice is saved apart from the other saves (`outbreak.analytics-consent`); old saves with no answer count as on. A small Privacy link sits beside the title's copyright line, and Options links to the same Privacy page (`privacy-text.ts`). Turning it off sets Google's `ga-disable` flag and clears the `_ga` cookies where the browser allows (`cookies.ts`). Nothing is sent from localhost, 127.0.0.1 or a dev build, which log `[analytics] would send` instead. `catalog.ts` is the allow-list of events and parameters: a value not on its list is dropped, and page addresses are sent without their query. One listener (`listener.ts`, started from `App.vue`) feeds `run-tracker.ts`, which turns screens, the HUD and the game's events into one summary per level or run. The game reaches it only through three one-line taps (`event-tap.ts`, called from `run-hud.ts` and `bench-link.ts`), which cannot change play. Title pages are counted by `TITLE_PAGE_EVENTS` in `run-tracker.ts`: add a page there and call `trackTitlePage` when it opens. The words of the Privacy page are in `privacy-text.ts` and must change with any change to what is sent; they have not been reviewed by anyone qualified.
- **Install and offline**: the game is an installable app (`public/manifest.webmanifest`, icons drawn from the favicon's zombie head by `tools/pwa-icons.ts`, with no studio name on them). Every path is relative, so it also works under a subfolder. The service worker is the hand-written `public/sw.js`, registered only in a production build (`src/game/pwa/worker-update.ts`), never on a dev server. `tools/vite-pwa.ts` stamps it at build time with a build id and the list of built files, so each build is a new worker and old caches are removed on activate. The shell is precached, so solo play opens offline; pages go network first with the cached page as fallback, and hashed assets are cache first. Music is not precached: a track is kept the first time it plays (at most 12, oldest dropped, stored one at a time so concurrent first plays cannot overshoot), range requests are answered from the kept copy (a multi-range request gets the whole file), a kept track is keyed by the build's hash of its file (`MUSIC_VERSIONS`, stamped by `tools/vite-pwa.ts`) so a replaced file is fetched again while unchanged tracks stay kept and are pruned on activate when replaced or removed, only a URL without a query is kept in the shell cache, before a waiting version takes over the page tells the worker in charge to abort its music streams (`RELEASE_MUSIC`) so a paused stream cannot hold the swap up for minutes, and a track that was never kept just fails and the game plays on with its generated bed. Only same-origin GET requests are touched, so Google Analytics and co-op sockets pass straight through. A new version waits and is applied only while the title screen shows, never during a run, or at the next launch; the title's state is remembered (`createUpdateHost`), so it counts whether the title mounted before or after the worker started, and an update on a first visit reloads too. Install Game is a pixel cartridge at the side of the title (`InstallCartridge.vue`, art in `src/art/cartridge.ts`, label in the magazine's paper colours from `paper-palette.css` and its `mag-color-tomato` band). `src/game/pwa/install-offer.ts` decides when it shows: only when the browser can install and the game is not installed. Chrome, Edge and Android use the held `beforeinstallprompt`; iPhone and iPad Safari open a short magazine-style card (`IosInstallCard.vue`, words in `ios-guide.ts`) for Share, then Add to Home Screen. A dismissed prompt keeps the cartridge, which then opens a card pointing at the browser menu. The cartridge is out of the flow (so the menu never jumps when the prompt arrives) and is hidden on screens too short to hold it clear of the menu and the credit line; the menu's Enter and arrow keys leave a focused control outside the menu, such as the cartridge, to answer for itself. It never shows during a run, and analytics sends `install_prompt` and `app_installed` through `catalog.ts`.

## Running locally

1. Install dependencies from the repository root:
   ```bash
   pnpm install
   ```

2. Start the client (default port 8096; set `ZOMBIE_CLIENT_PORT` to change it):
   ```bash
   pnpm --filter zombie-survival dev
   ```

3. For co-op, also start the server (default port 8097; set `ZOMBIE_PORT` to change it):
   ```bash
   pnpm --filter zombie-survival dev:server
   ```

   `ZOMBIE_PORT` must be the same value for the client and the server: the client uses it as the default server address on the setup page. To run on other ports, for example 8196 and 8197:
   ```bash
   ZOMBIE_CLIENT_PORT=8196 ZOMBIE_PORT=8197 pnpm --filter zombie-survival dev
   ZOMBIE_PORT=8197 pnpm --filter zombie-survival dev:server
   ```

4. Open `http://localhost:8096` (or your `ZOMBIE_CLIENT_PORT`). Choose Solo, or enter the server address and choose Co-op in each browser window.
